# Internal report (research report, findings memo, landscape scan, diligence summary)

**Reader.** One named internal reader (a founder, an executive, a lead engineer) mining the document for what they need to know. They know their own field cold and will not recognize niche organizations, insider shorthand, or trade-press bylines from outside it. They may read it on a phone.

**Purpose.** Transfer findings to that reader as fast and as plainly as possible. Plainness here is respect for a reader who is extracting, not savoring; the wit that belongs in a relationship memo stays out of a findings report.

**Spine.** Explainer, in a five-part shape for "what is the world saying about X", adaptable in the middle for other findings:

1. Title states the literal question the report answers ("Who has critiqued Project X"), never the strategic spin.
2. Bottom line, one paragraph: the finding, then the opening or implication it creates, then stop. It must stand alone, with jargon removed.
3. The landscape: one plain intro line describing what the table is, then the table (`Source | Author | Title | Summary | Date`, summaries four sentences or fewer, ordered on one axis, no ranking or confidence column; a weight or caveat goes inline in the cell as words).
4. Common ground and the opening, very short: what every source shares, then the novel contribution open to us, ending on the substance.
5. Sources: a numbered list in table order with author, title, publication, date, working URL. The report is self-contained.

**Voice heuristics.** Strip performance (metaphor where a plain statement works, staccato fragment closers, "three things keep the ground open" scaffolding); gloss every organization and term inline exactly where the reader would mis-weight it ("Example Institute (small think tank)"), leaving alone names that self-signal; plainest at the top, full technical detail deeper down; cut the "here's what I excluded and why" apparatus; cut self-justifying closers; prefer honest softening over false precision ("all publications that have made something similar to the X argument").

**Review floor.** Readers: one cold read for the audience-fit pass, plus a claim-by-claim check against the sources for any number or quotation.

**Final pass.** Read the bottom line alone: does it stand? Scan the table: every niche organization marked, no ranking column, summaries at four sentences or fewer. Confirm the title states the question.
