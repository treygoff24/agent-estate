# Cold email (someone who does not know you: a prospect, a maintainer, a researcher, a potential partner)

**Reader.** One busy person who does not know you, scanning on a phone between meetings, deciding in ten seconds whether this is a scheduling note or a pitch.

**Controlling idea.** The email gets the meeting; the one-pager and the conversation do the persuading. Every sentence either builds warmth, builds credibility, or makes the yes easier. Default 100 to 150 words; one substantive paragraph earns its place only when the hook is a verbatim quote from the recipient's own public record.

**Spine.** Ask. Greeting matched to the relationship; the connect (how you know each other or who routed you) before any substance; the hook in their words, strongest as the question they are already asking; the credibility line (one clause of who you are and what you run, for a true cold); the ask, feedback-framed for a peer ("share the evolved version and get your feedback on it"), a straight meeting ask for a decision-maker; a concrete window plus a fallback ("the morning of Tuesday the 16th? If not, the week of the 22nd?"), both windows confirmed with the sender before sending; "I've attached a one-pager for a quick overview"; a plain sign-off, with the title block only on true colds.

**Guardrails.** Do not name third parties who have not agreed to be named; naming one is the sender's call, flagged, never made for them. Check the inbox first: a live thread changes register and owner. Verify the address. One relationship per email, one ask, no side offers, no strategy narrated, no agenda front-loaded, no off-ramp that invites a no. Sends are the owner's, never the agent's.

**Review floor.** Lint, then one cold read when the email carries a claim the sender has not made before.

**Worked example:**

> Subject: Your retry-budget post & the library it needs
>
> Hi Jordan,
>
> Your post on retry budgets argues that client libraries should refuse to retry once the server signals overload. We maintain an open-source client that does the opposite by default, and we have spent a year measuring what that costs.
>
> When you described the thundering-herd failure at the conference talk in March, that matched what we saw in production: a three-minute brownout turning into forty.
>
> A one-page overview of the fix is attached.
>
> I'm in town Tuesday the 16th and flexible after. Worth 20 minutes to walk through how it fits your proposal? Happy to come by the office.
>
> Best,
> Alex
