# Beats: a journey through the pile, one beat at a time

The author passes a markdown file of raw material. The exploring is done and the pile is fixed; commit to a path through it and mine the pile to fill each beat. If the author did not say where to save the article, ask once and remember. Then run a beat-by-beat journey, choose-your-own-adventure style:

1. **Establish the prerequisites.** Settle what the audience knows walking in; everything else is grounded by a beat before a later beat uses it.
2. **Offer two or three candidate starting beats** drawn from the pile, each a different entry point, each leaning only on grounded concepts, each labeled with what it grounds. Show them before writing anything. The author picks one; preview what that pick unlocks a little way down the path.
3. **Write only that beat** to the article file and stop.
4. **Re-read the file from disk**, then offer two or three candidate next beats: different directions the journey could pivot to from where the article now stands, each reachable from the current grounded set, each labeled with what it grounds.
5. Loop until the article reaches a natural end.

## Grounding

Each beat requires concepts already grounded and grounds new ones. Keep the running list and update it as each beat lands. A candidate beat is reachable only if everything it requires is grounded; picking a beat that grounds X unlocks every beat waiting on X, and that is what shapes the adventure. When a tempting beat requires something nothing has grounded, the fix is a grounding beat before it or promoting the concept to a prerequisite.

## What a beat is

One move: sets a scene, lands a point, asks a question, drops an aside, twists the angle. Then it stops, leaving the reader where the next beat can pivot. It is sized by what it needs: a single sentence ("And then nothing happened for three weeks."), a short paragraph, or a self-contained vignette. A "beat" that needs five paragraphs and three subheadings is two beats glued together; split it.

## Ending

The article ends when the journey is complete, never when the pile is empty. Leftover fragments are the point of having more raw material than you need.

## Rhythm

One beat at a time; never write ahead. Re-read from disk before every write and preserve his edits absolutely; a substantial edit to an earlier beat changes what comes next. "Rewrite that beat" or "try a different beat 3" edits in place and leaves the rest alone.
